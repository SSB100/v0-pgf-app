# Waypoint Journey Curriculum Review

## Purpose

This review maps the workbook material supplied by the founder into Waypoint's self-guided Journey. The source material is being used as curriculum context and as a prompt for coverage, sequencing and exercise design. Waypoint does not reproduce the workbook as a digital copy.

The handwritten responses visible in some scans are **not product content**. They were reviewed only as examples of the kind of reflection the original exercises were designed to elicit.

## Design decision: one Journey, not separate Skills and Training libraries

The previous product split learning across `/journey`, `/skills` and `/training`. That made the content feel like separate features and left several important ideas disconnected from the skills they were meant to prepare people to use.

The rebuilt Journey uses a consistent pattern:

1. **Learn** — explain the idea in enough detail to understand what the skill is for and what it is not for.
2. **Check** — one short knowledge check with feedback to reinforce the key distinction.
3. **Practise** — one small application exercise. A user can choose to reflect privately instead of typing personal details into Waypoint.

Free-text practice answers in these new guided modules are not sent to the Journey completion API. The API records module completion only.

## Source structure reviewed

### Wellness / change foundations workbook

The supplied scans cover:

- introduction and identifying the behaviour or problem to change
- motivation, readiness for change and incentives/barriers
- decisional balance: advantages and disadvantages of staying the same and changing
- commitment and purpose
- chain analysis
- managing complex change through vision, skills, incentives, resources and an action plan
- a broad wellbeing model including safety, stability, choice, meaning, purpose, connection, identity, health, learning, autonomy, contribution, activity, creativity and self-control
- the principles of Acceptance, Authenticity and Action
- disconnection, relationships and support
- personal strengths and external resources
- committing to a plan, barriers, supports and a final reflection

### Skills workbook

The supplied contents page and scans cover four broad areas:

**Core Mindfulness Skills**
- mindfulness foundations
- Observe, Describe and Participate
- Non-judgementally, One-Mindfully and Effectively
- Five-Things external grounding
- mindfulness of breath
- urge surfing
- Reasonable Mind, Emotional Mind and Wise Mind
- RAIN

**Emotional Awareness**
- functions and components of emotions
- understanding an emotional experience
- action urges and Opposite Action
- emotion vocabulary / emotion wheel
- common unhelpful thinking patterns
- Check the Facts
- ABC PLEASE
- accumulating positive experiences
- building mastery
- coping ahead
- body-care / PLEASE reminders and sleep-related routines

**Tolerating Life's Distress Effectively**
- STOP
- TIP
- compassion and compassion practice
- ACCEPTS
- IMPROVE and its planning worksheet
- reality acceptance
- willingness
- Turning the Mind
- six-step problem solving and worksheet

**Fostering Relationships and Connection**
- interpersonal effectiveness overview
- objective, relationship and self-respect effectiveness
- DEAR MAN
- GIVE
- FAST

### Life Garden / values material

The separate Life Garden material uses progressive elimination to force prioritisation among meaningful life areas. Waypoint's onboarding now performs that narrowing interaction. The Journey values module revisits the result and makes an important distinction: eliminating an item during prioritisation does **not** mean that area or value is unimportant.

## New Waypoint curriculum

The rebuilt Journey contains **27 guided modules**.

### 1. Getting Started

1. **Understanding the Pattern**
   - maps the workbook's problem-identification and contextual reflection material
   - separates a specific behaviour from identity-based labels
   - looks at both short-term function and longer-term cost

2. **Motivation for Change**
   - incorporates readiness/stages language as a reflection model rather than a rigid progression
   - includes decisional balance and ambivalence
   - avoids treating mixed motivation as failure

3. **Chain Analysis**
   - maps vulnerabilities, prompting events, thoughts, emotions, urges, behaviour and consequences
   - finishes by finding practical interruption points rather than blame

4. **Wellbeing, Acceptance, Authenticity & Action**
   - integrates the workbook's wider wellbeing framework and three principles
   - positions acceptance as accurately recognising the starting point, not approval or surrender

### 2. Mindfulness & Awareness

5. **Mindfulness Foundations**
   - Observe, Describe, Participate
   - Non-judgementally, One-Mindfully, Effectively
   - clarifies that mindfulness does not require passivity

6. **Understanding Your Mind**
   - Emotional Mind, Reasonable Mind, Wise Mind
   - presents these as DBT reflection concepts rather than fixed personality types

7. **Grounding, Breath, RAIN & Urge Surfing**
   - combines related present-moment practices into one coherent module
   - includes external grounding when inward focus is not helpful
   - makes clear that urge surfing does not guarantee an urge will disappear

8. **Building Daily Awareness**
   - connects mindfulness to Waypoint's day-to-day check-ins
   - distinguishes observation from interpretation and clinical inference

9. **Recognising Triggers & Vulnerabilities**
   - distinguishes immediate cues from background vulnerability factors
   - covers both internal and external cues

10. **Choice Points: Towards & Away Moves**
    - connects awareness with values-based action
    - avoids implying that people control every circumstance or outcome

### 3. Emotions & Responses

11. **Understanding Emotions**
    - covers subjective experience, body response, expression, interpretation and action urges
    - frames emotions as potentially informative without treating them as automatically factual

12. **Thinking Patterns & Check the Facts**
    - includes the thinking patterns covered across the source pages
    - separates observations from predictions, judgements and interpretations
    - links the result to problem solving, acceptance or Opposite Action

13. **Opposite Action**
    - restores more teaching around emotion/action-urge relationships
    - requires checking facts and safety before acting opposite

14. **ABC PLEASE: Reducing Vulnerability & Building a Life**
    - combines PLEASE, accumulating positive experiences and building mastery
    - uses flexible wellbeing prompts rather than rigid health rules
    - includes medication and withdrawal safety cautions

15. **Coping Ahead**
    - planning and mental rehearsal for predictable difficult situations
    - includes a trauma/distress caution rather than encouraging forced exposure

### 4. Values & Direction

16. **Discovering Your Values**
    - revisits the Life Garden outcome from onboarding
    - distinguishes values, goals and perfectionistic rules

17. **Recognising Your Strengths & Resources**
    - uses evidence from previous change to identify strengths
    - explicitly counts appropriate external help as a resource rather than a weakness

18. **From Values to Committed Action**
    - converts one value into a specific activity and realistic first goal
    - adds barrier planning and connection/support

### 5. Distress & Problem Solving

19. **Creating Space: STOP & TIP**
    - places the two fast-response skills together
    - adds medical caution around cold exposure and intense exercise
    - links psychological pause skills with practical environmental safeguards

20. **Distress Tolerance Toolkit: ACCEPTS & IMPROVE**
    - combines the source workbook's temporary distraction and moment-improvement options
    - includes compassion as a stance toward distress rather than punishment or self-attack
    - preserves the supplied workbook's adapted IMPROVE version: Imagery, Meaning, **Planning**, Relaxing, One Thing, Vacation and Encouragement

21. **Reality Acceptance, Willingness & Turning the Mind**
    - combines three concepts that build directly on one another
    - removes coercive interpretations of acceptance or willingness
    - explicitly preserves safety, boundaries, legal rights and available action

22. **Six-Step Problem Solving**
    - define, brainstorm, compare, plan, act and evaluate
    - treats an unsuccessful first plan as information rather than failure

### 6. Relationships & Connection

23. **Interpersonal Effectiveness**
    - teaches the three competing goals before teaching the acronyms:
      - objective effectiveness
      - relationship effectiveness
      - self-respect effectiveness

24. **DEAR MAN**
    - request/refusal/boundary structure
    - explicitly rejects continuing a communication script in threatening, violent or coercive situations

25. **GIVE**
    - relationship effectiveness through gentleness, interest, validation and an easy manner
    - clarifies that validation is not agreement or responsibility for another person's reaction

26. **FAST**
    - fairness, appropriate apologies, values and truthfulness
    - clarifies that truthfulness does not require unsafe disclosure

### 7. Putting It Together

27. **Your Personal Commitment Plan**
    - combines direction/vision, skills, incentives, resources, support and action planning
    - incorporates recurrence planning without turning a recurrence into an identity or erasing prior learning
    - includes both protective distance from risky environments and repair of supportive connection where appropriate

## Topics consolidated rather than made into separate modules

Not every workbook heading needs its own screen. Several closely related topics were intentionally combined so the Journey teaches connected ideas together rather than becoming a long list of acronyms.

- **Five-Things grounding, breath mindfulness, RAIN and urge surfing** are combined in one present-moment skills module.
- **Emotion wheel / emotion vocabulary** is covered within Understanding Emotions rather than as a standalone module.
- **Accumulating positive experiences, building mastery and PLEASE** are combined as ABC PLEASE.
- **Compassion / compassion practice** is integrated into RAIN and the distress-tolerance toolkit. It is framed as responding to distress with care while retaining accountability and appropriate action.
- **Sleep hygiene** sits inside the physical-foundations section of ABC PLEASE rather than being a standalone treatment protocol.
- **ACCEPTS planning and IMPROVE planning worksheets** become interactive prompts inside the relevant module.
- **Willingness and Turning the Mind** sit directly inside Reality Acceptance because the source material presents them as part of the same acceptance process.
- **Interpersonal overview** is taught before DEAR MAN, GIVE and FAST so users understand which communication goal each acronym serves.

## Important fidelity and safety distinctions

Waypoint should not describe this Journey as a complete DBT programme or as DBT treatment. It contains DBT-informed skills, but comprehensive DBT is broader than a self-guided app and includes clinician-led treatment modes, coaching and structured behavioural work.

The Journey should also avoid claiming the supplied workbook is proof that Waypoint itself is evidence-based or clinically validated. A source programme may draw on established therapies, but Waypoint's specific delivery, wording, sequence and interaction design would need its own professional review and evaluation.

The stages-of-change material is retained as a useful way to discuss readiness, while avoiding a claim that everyone progresses through clean, mutually exclusive stages in a fixed order.

## Content ownership / provenance approach

- Do not copy handwritten responses into the product.
- Do not reproduce substantial workbook passages, worksheets, illustrations or page layouts.
- Use original Waypoint explanations, scenarios, questions and interaction design.
- Maintain attribution/provenance internally for concepts derived from established therapeutic approaches.
- Before a formal pilot, complete a professional therapeutic-content review and maintain a versioned evidence/provenance register for each module.

## Recommended next review

Before merging this rebuild into a pilot branch, review the 27-module sequence for:

- reading load and mobile usability
- whether the length of each module feels manageable
- accessibility and plain-language comprehension
- clinical fidelity of DBT/ACT/CBT terminology
- lived-experience acceptability, especially shame, accountability and recurrence language
- cultural fit for Aotearoa and opportunities for Māori and Pacific co-design
- whether any practice should be saved deliberately in a future journal feature rather than keeping all exercise text ephemeral
