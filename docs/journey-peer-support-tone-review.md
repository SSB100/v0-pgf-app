# Journey peer-support tone review

## Purpose

This pass reviews all 27 guided Journey modules for how they sound to a person who may be tired, ashamed, anxious, distracted, low in hope or simply not interested in reading clinician-facing material.

The goal is not to make the material casual or less accurate. It is to make it feel like a useful person is walking beside the user and explaining an idea in ordinary language.

## Wording principles

- Lead with the human situation before the therapy term or acronym.
- Use second-person language carefully and avoid telling the user what they must feel.
- Explain concepts through everyday examples such as payday, arguments, unanswered messages, avoiding bills, isolating, urges, parenting, work stress and ordinary routines.
- Use analogies when they make the idea easier to remember, but do not force an analogy into every lesson.
- Keep shame and character judgement out of the teaching. Describe behaviour, context and consequences instead.
- Treat relapse or recurrence as information without pretending consequences do not matter.
- Keep safety boundaries explicit around trauma, withdrawal, physical TIP strategies, coercion, violence and genuine danger.
- Keep formal terms such as Wise Mind, DEAR MAN, GIVE, FAST, PLEASE, ACCEPTS and IMPROVE where they are useful, but explain the ordinary-language idea first.
- Exercises should sound like reflection with a person, not homework from a workbook.

## Examples of the new approach

- Chain Analysis is introduced as replaying a moment in slow motion and uses a payday-to-gambling chain as an example.
- Awareness is framed as a weather report rather than a school report.
- Triggers and vulnerabilities use the spark-and-dry-grass analogy.
- Emotions are described as dashboard lights rather than steering wheels.
- Check the Facts uses a camera-versus-narrator test.
- Coping Ahead uses the idea of taking an umbrella before it rains.
- Values are a compass rather than a perfection standard.
- Strengths are identified through evidence rather than asking the user to declare themselves a strong person.
- Reality Acceptance is framed as putting your feet on the ground that already exists without approving of what happened.
- Problem Solving asks the user to untangle one knot instead of solving their whole life.
- The final commitment plan is written for the difficult day rather than the user's most motivated day.

## Full context on screen

The previous renderer showed the first sentence of each teaching section and hid the rest behind **More context**. That made the first exposure too thin and could make the module feel like a series of slogans.

The toggle has been removed. Each screen still contains only one teaching idea, but the full explanation and example for that idea are visible immediately.

## Knowledge checks

Knowledge checks remain learning tools rather than tests.

After any answer:

1. Waypoint explains why the selected answer fits or does not fit.
2. If the user selected a different answer, Waypoint also displays **The answer to take with you**, including the ideal answer and its explanation.

A wrong first choice therefore becomes additional teaching rather than leaving the user with only a message that their answer was wrong.

## Scope

The peer-support wording is applied through the Journey presentation layer so the fuller curriculum remains available as the source of record. Existing safety notes and module structure remain intact.
